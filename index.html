FAMILY ORAL HISTORY

Iya Alaro: A legacy across generations

According to her family's account, Iya Alaro, also known as Safiyat Ogun Dupe, came from Osun State and worked as a herbal trader. Her journeys brought her to Amoo Street in Ojota, where she later established a home. Her descendants remember her generosity, influence and association with the landmark called Iya Alaro Bridge.

Iya Alaro

The family identifies the elderly woman in patterned clothing in its photographs as Iya Alaro. Her exact birth and death dates remain unknown.

Her daughter carries the tradition forward

The woman in white traditional apparel, red beads and ceremonial headwear is Iya Alaro's daughter — not a younger photograph of Iya Alaro. According to the family, she continues the tradition today.

Community memory

The family preserves accounts of traditional observances associated with Iya Alaro Bridge. The bridge's naming history and a reported lifespan of 138 years have not been independently verified.

Our research standard: family testimony is attributed as oral history, not presented as independently verified archival fact. The family has confirmed permission for public use of the supplied photographs. The original photographs will appear here when their image files are added to the site. Any AI reconstructions will be clearly labeled.
