OJOTA CULTURAL HERITAGE

Ajeran: The Pride of Ojota

According to Ojota’s oral history, Ajeran was the first masquerade to enter Ojota land. Its colourful costume, commanding presence, and energetic movements have made it a treasured symbol of the community’s cultural heritage.

Ajeran is associated in the community’s oral tradition with the four forefathers, led by Chief Gafar. Their legacy connects generations and helps preserve the stories and customs of Ojota.

Community accounts describe Ajeran as one of Ojota’s most popular and powerful masquerades. Before its appearance, custodians prepare traditional items and, according to the oral history shared by some residents, a goat offering. These details are presented as oral tradition and should be preserved with respect for the people who carry the story.

Whenever Ajeran appears, drummers play, crowds gather, and excitement fills the streets. Its grand appearance is a celebration of a tradition passed down through generations.

Before the Appearance

Traditional preparations begin before Ajeran makes its grand appearance. Elders and custodians arrange calabashes, herbs, kola nuts, and other customary items with solemn respect, while drummers prepare and the community waits.

An Offering in the Oral Tradition

According to the oral tradition shared by some people of Ojota, a goat is prepared as an offering before Ajeran appears. This account is presented as community heritage and oral history.

The Grand Appearance

Golden fringes sway as the red, blue, green, yellow, and white costume moves majestically. Drums thunder, the crowd responds with excitement, and the streets become a stage for cultural celebration.

A Legacy Across Generations

More than a masquerade, Ajeran represents a treasured story of community, identity, memory, and cultural pride—one that Ojota’s people can document and pass on to future generations.
